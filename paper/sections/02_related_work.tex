\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}
\paragraph{Continuation and curricula.}
Classical continuation methods trace solutions through parameterized problems \citep{allgower1990continuation}. For nonconvex optimization, \citet{mobahi2015gaussian} analyze Gaussian-smoothed objectives, while \citet{hazan2016graduated} identify a class for which graduated optimization converges globally. Spatial filtering inside a network is not equivalent to smoothing its loss in parameter space, so these guarantees do not directly apply here. Related approaches include noisy activations and network deformations in Mollifying Networks \citep{gulcehre2016mollifying}, and curricula that progressively change which examples contribute to training \citep{bengio2009curriculum}. Our interventions keep the examples and labels fixed and change their spatial processing.

\paragraph{Spatial information during training.}
Curriculum by Smoothing (CBS) \citep{sinha2020curriculum} is our direct starting point: Gaussian kernels filter feature maps, and their scale decreases during training. Changing input resolution is also established. \citet{hoffer2019mixmatch} mix image sizes during training, while \citet{tan2021efficientnetv2} combine progressive resizing with adaptive regularization. A particularly close precedent is SDPoint \citep{kuen2018stochastic}, which applies average-pooling downsampling at a randomly selected location and ratio. Its instances share parameters and provide different inference costs. Our resolution procedure fixes the insertion point and progressively restores the native grid, ending at the original inference architecture. We study it alongside two selected filtering procedures. These are CBS-inspired configurations, not a reproduction of CBS; we have not yet run a direct comparison against CBS or SDPoint.

\paragraph{Frequency and aliasing.}
\citet{rahaman2019spectral} study a bias towards low-frequency functions during neural-network learning. Frequencies of the learned function in input space differ, however, from spatial frequencies within a feature map. Downsampling also differs from low-pass filtering. Filtering before subsampling can improve shift consistency \citep{zhang2019shift}, and its placement affects generalization \citep{vasconcelos2021aliasing}. Nonlinearities can introduce further frequency components and aliasing \citep{michaeli2023aliasfree}. A blur after a strided convolution therefore does not undo aliasing already introduced by that convolution.

\paragraph{Geometry and sensitivity.}
The selected procedures end with the same trainable parameter shapes, but their optimization paths differ. \citet{li2018visualizing} use filter-normalized directions and trajectory projections to visualize neural-network losses. Reparameterization can change apparent sharpness without changing the predictor \citep{dinh2017sharp}; a flatter slice alone is not a generalization explanation. Hessian--vector products \citep{pearlmutter1994hessian} allow local spectral measurements without storing a dense Hessian, as used in large-scale curvature studies \citep{ghorbani2019hessian}. Input Jacobians provide a complementary, function-space diagnostic and enter margin-based generalization analyses \citep{sokolic2017robust}. Our measurements are descriptive: we do not derive a bound from them or identify a causal mechanism.
