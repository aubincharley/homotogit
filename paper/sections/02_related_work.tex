\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}
\paragraph{Continuation and curricula.}
\citet{hazan2016graduated} study objectives smoothed over progressively smaller neighborhoods and characterize a class for which graduated optimization converges globally. Their assumptions are not established for the CNN losses considered here. Curriculum learning instead changes which examples contribute to training, often by introducing harder examples progressively \citep{bengio2009curriculum}. Our interventions keep the examples and labels fixed and change the network through which they are processed. Spatial simplicity is an intuition for constructing the family, not a guarantee that its loss is easier to optimize.

\paragraph{Spatial information during training.}
The closest starting point is Curriculum by Smoothing \citep{sinha2020curriculum}, which convolves feature maps with Gaussian kernels and decreases their scale during training. Progressive image size is also an established training strategy: \citet{tan2021efficientnetv2} combine increasing input resolution with changing regularization in EfficientNetV2. We focus on temporary reduction at an internal feature map and retain the same parameterized network at the end of training. This is a focused empirical comparison within that broader line of work, rather than a claim that increasing resolution is a new curriculum principle.

\paragraph{Frequency and aliasing.}
\citet{rahaman2019spectral} study a bias towards low-frequency functions during neural-network learning. This is conceptually related to our motivation, but the frequencies of a learned function in input space are not the spatial frequencies of a feature map. Moreover, lowering resolution and low-pass filtering are different operations. \citet{zhang2019shift} introduce filtering before subsampling to improve shift consistency, and \citet{vasconcelos2021aliasing} examine how the placement of such filters affects generalization. Nonlinearities can also generate frequency components that require anti-aliasing treatment \citep{michaeli2023aliasfree}. These works make filter placement relevant, but do not imply that every beneficial blur is acting through anti-aliasing. A filter placed after a strided convolution cannot undo the aliasing already introduced by that convolution.

\paragraph{Implicit regularization and loss geometry.}
All three selected procedures end in the same architecture, so their effect cannot be explained by a different final parameter count. Optimization and parameterization can nevertheless influence which functions are learned \citep{neyshabur2017geometry}. Early-stopping analyses make this distinction explicit in least-squares models \citep{ali2019early}, while stability provides another route from an algorithm to generalization \citep{hardt2016stability}. For empirical geometry, \citet{li2018visualizing} use filter-normalized directions and trajectory projections to visualize neural-network losses. We adopt these tools as diagnostics. Since reparameterization can change apparent sharpness without changing the predictor \citep{dinh2017sharp}, a visually flatter surface alone would not explain improved generalization.
