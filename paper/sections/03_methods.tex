\section{Spatial continuation}
\label{sec:methods}
Let $\mathcal D=\{(x_i,y_i)\}_{i=1}^n$ be the training sample and $f_\theta$ the original network. An intervention state $s$ specifies a spatial resolution and the scales of any added filters. It defines
\begin{equation}
 L_s(\theta)=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\ell(f_{\theta,s}(x_i),y_i),
 \qquad f_{\theta,s_\star}=f_\theta,
 \label{eq:family}
\end{equation}
where $\ell$ is cross-entropy and $s_\star$ the final state. We take a finite number of stochastic gradient updates at each state and retain the weights and optimizer state across transitions. We do not solve each intermediate problem exactly. Since resolutions are discrete and max-pooling is nonsmooth, ``continuation'' describes a staged training procedure, not a smooth path with a proved minimizer-tracking guarantee. BatchNorm statistics are specified separately.

\subsection{Resolution reduction}
For a feature map $h\in\R^{C\times H\times W}$, adaptive max-pooling returns a smaller map:
\begin{equation}
 [R_{r_h,r_w}(h)]_{c,i,j}=\max_{(p,q)\in B_{i,j}}h_{c,p,q}.
 \label{eq:pool}
\end{equation}
The windows $B_{i,j}$ are defined in Appendix~\ref{app:sites}. In the selected ResNet-20, we reduce the complete output of the second residual block, after its residual addition and final ReLU. Both branches of the next block receive the pooled tensor. Its side length follows $16\to24\to32$ over epochs 0--5, 6--11 and 12--29. Later layers process the smaller tensors directly; there is no immediate upsampling. Global average pooling preserves the classifier input dimension. The trainable parameter shapes are unchanged. Max-pooling keeps local maxima; it is not a linear low-pass projection.

\subsection{Gaussian filtering}
At an insertion site $j$, we replace a feature map by
\begin{equation}
 h_{j,s}=G_{\sigma_j(s)}*h_j,
 \label{eq:gaussian}
\end{equation}
using a normalized Gaussian applied separately to each channel. In the reference setting, it is separable with nine taps per axis and reflection padding. The filters have no trainable parameters and are bypassed exactly at scale zero. The Gaussian-only method filters ten post-ReLU outputs: the stem and the nine residual blocks. The activation inside each block is not filtered. The scale $g(e)$ decreases by three-epoch plateaus, from $1$ to $0.3$, and is zero from epoch 21.

The combined procedure uses the same reduction as \methodR{}, but places the Gaussian at all 19 convolution outputs, before BatchNorm. Its effective scale is
\begin{equation}
 \sigma_j(e)=g(e)\frac{r(e)}{32}\quad\text{at all 19 sites},

including the five sites upstream of the reduction, whose maps remain $32\times32$. This executed rule produces some upward jumps in the effective scale when resolution increases (Appendix~\ref{app:schedules}). Consequently, \methodRG{} is not the literal composition of the selected \methodR{} and \methodG{} presets: the Gaussian placement and scale differ. The comparison concerns three selected procedures, not a strict factorial decomposition of two independent interventions. All finish at the native grid with the added operations bypassed.
