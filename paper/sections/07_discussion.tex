% The current evidence supports a practical observation: a temporary internal reduction, implemented with simple max-pooling, improves the outcome of the tested short training procedure. Gaussian filtering can add a further gain, but its placement interacts with the resolution intervention: without reduction, post-ReLU filtering scored above filtering at convolution outputs, and with reduction the order was reversed. In the final batch, the combined procedure scored above resolution reduction alone in each of the three seeds, by $\uCombMinusRes$ points on average, while \uNearCombCount{} other reduction-plus-Gaussian variants finished within 0.6 points of it. Three seeds on a reused test set do not single out one operator. The combination also costs more time, with a mean wall time of $\uWallComb$\,s per run against $\uWallPlain$\,s for the baseline, evaluation included. The exploration does not establish a universal ordering of operators, an asymptotic improvement or a theoretical guarantee. The remaining work is to test the selected procedures beyond the reference setting and to determine which statistical and geometric observations help explain their behavior.
