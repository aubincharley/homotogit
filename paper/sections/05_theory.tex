\section{A statistical interpretation}
\label{sec:theory}
The observation we would like to explain is a higher training cross-entropy together with a lower test cross-entropy at the end of the same training budget. At epoch 30, on a fixed probe of 500 training images, the cross-entropy is $\uProbeRes$, $\uProbeGauss$ and $\uProbeComb$ for the resolution, Gaussian and combined procedures, against $\uProbePlain$ for the baseline, while the test cross-entropy is $\uTestCERes$, $\uTestCEGauss$ and $\uTestCEComb$ against $\uTestCEPlain$ (means over three seeds, Table~\ref{tab:final-losses}). The mean loss recorded during the last epoch shows the same ordering, but the probe is small and the full training loss of the final weights was not evaluated. One possible interpretation is that delaying access to some information changes the balance between fitting the signal and fitting sample-specific variation. This would be an effect of the optimization trajectory, since the temporary operators are absent from the final model.

% A tractable first setting is a regression model with components that become available at different times. The objective would be to identify conditions under which delayed fitting increases empirical error while decreasing expected prediction error. Early-stopping analyses provide a useful starting point \citep{ali2019early}. Such a result would illustrate a mechanism under stated signal and noise assumptions; it would not by itself prove the explanation for nonlinear max-pooling, BatchNorm or classification cross-entropy. Likewise, a smaller upper bound on risk is not sufficient to order two actual risks.
