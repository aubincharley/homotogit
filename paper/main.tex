\runningtitle{Training CNNs with Spatial Continuation}
\runningauthor{Charley, Corrard, El khamlichi, Nicaise}
\twocolumn[
\aistatstitle{Training Convolutional Networks with Spatial Continuation}
\aistatsauthor{Aubin Charley \And Alexandre Corrard \And Idriss El khamlichi \And Maxime Nicaise}
\aistatsaddress{CentraleSup\'elec, Universit\'e Paris-Saclay}
]
\begin{abstract}
An idea to train a neural network is to start from a simpler problem and progressively recover the original one. We investigate this idea by changing the spatial information processed by convolutional networks during training. We compare temporary resolution reduction, Gaussian filtering of feature maps, and their combination, all ending at the original inference architecture. An exploratory CIFAR-10 study selects three procedures, which we then examine across datasets, architectures and optimizers. Resolution reduction improves mean test accuracy in all twelve benchmark settings, including the selection setting, while the additional benefit of filtering depends on the training recipe. On STL-10, the combination performs below the baseline although either intervention alone improves it. A five-seed analysis on CIFAR-10 finds smaller leading Hessian eigenvalues for all three procedures with fixed BatchNorm statistics. Finite perturbations with recalibrated statistics give a different ordering for Gaussian filtering alone. These observations support a benefit of temporary spatial interventions in the tested regimes, but do not establish a common geometric explanation or a generalization guarantee.
\end{abstract}
